# How to read a settlement as a leased owner-operator

If you lease your truck to a carrier, you get a settlement, not a regular paystub. It shows what you earned, then what was taken out. Here is how to read it.

## The top part: what you earned

Each load is a line. It may show:

- The load or trip number
- The total the carrier says it was paid for the load
- Your share. This may be a percent of the load or a rate per mile.
- Extra pay, like stops, detention or fuel surcharge, if your lease shares it

Check: Take your lease. Find your share. Do the math for each load.
Made-up example: The load paid $2,000.00. Your lease says 70 percent. Your share is $1,400.00.

## The middle part: money taken out

This is where most of the lines are. Common ones:

- Fuel, if you used the carrier's fuel card
- Insurance
- Truck or trailer payments
- Plates and permits
- Escrow (money held back for later, see our escrow guide)
- Advances you took during the week

Check: Is each one in your lease? Is each amount what your lease or your receipts say? Is any line there twice?

## The bottom part: what you get

Earned minus taken out. This is what goes to your bank.

## Things to ask for

Your lease may let you see the paper that shows what the carrier was paid for a load, often called the rate confirmation. If your pay is a percent of the load, this is how you check the starting number. Read your lease to see what it says.

## If a line does not match

Write down the load, the line, your number and where it came from. Ask the carrier's settlement office. Keep a copy.
