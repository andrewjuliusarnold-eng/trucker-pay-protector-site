# Paid by the mile: how to check your miles and rate

If you are paid by the mile, the miles line is often the biggest line on your stub. A small gap in miles can add up over a year. Here is how to check it.

## Know which miles you are paid for

Your pay plan should say how miles are counted. Common ways:

- Map miles. A mileage program counts the miles from stop to stop, often the shortest way.
- Practical miles. The miles a truck would really drive on normal truck roads.
- Hub or odometer miles. The miles your truck really drove.

These can be different for the same trip. Your plan decides which one you are paid for.

## Know your rate

Your rate may change with:

- How long you have been there
- The kind of load or run
- Loaded miles or empty miles

Write down each rate your plan gives you.

## How to check it

1. For each trip, write down the miles your plan counts. If your plan uses map miles, your trip papers or dispatch may show them.
2. Add up the miles for the week.
3. Multiply by your rate.

Made-up example: 2,400 miles times 55 cents a mile is $1,320.00.

4. Compare with the miles line and the rate on your stub.

## If it does not match

Check the miles first, then the rate. Write down each trip, the miles and the rate you used. Ask your payroll office. Keep a copy.
