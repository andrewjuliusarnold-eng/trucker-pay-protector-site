How to check your trucking paystub, line by line

Your paystub has a lot of small lines. Each one is a part of your pay. Here is a simple way to check them.

What you need

  • Your pay plan. This is the paper or page that says how you get paid. It may be called a pay sheet, pay plan or offer letter.
  • Your paystub for the week.
  • A record of your week. This can be your logbook, your trip papers, or notes you took each day.

Step 1: Write down your pay rules

Read your pay plan. Write down each way you get paid. For example:

  • A rate for each mile
  • Pay for each stop
  • Pay for waiting time (often called detention)
  • Pay for a night away from home or a layover
  • Any hourly pay

Write the rate next to each one.

Step 2: Write down what you did that week

From your record, count each thing:

  • How many miles
  • How many stops
  • How many hours you waited, and where
  • Any layover or breakdown

Step 3: Do the math for each line

For each pay rule, multiply what you did by the rate.
Made-up example: 2,000 miles times 50 cents a mile is $1,000.00.

Step 4: Compare each line with your stub

Put your number next to the stub's number. Mark each line:

  • Match
  • Does not match

Step 5: Check the money taken out

Look at each deduction: insurance, escrow, fuel, advances. Is each one on your papers? Is each amount what your papers say?

Step 6: If a line does not match

Write down the line, your number, the stub's number, and where your number came from.
Then ask your payroll office about it. Be polite and keep a copy.
Sometimes the stub is right and a rule works in a way you did not expect. Asking is how you find out.
