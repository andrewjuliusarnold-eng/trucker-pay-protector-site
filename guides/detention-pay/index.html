What detention pay is, and how to check it on your stub

Detention pay is pay for waiting. You get to a shipper or receiver, and you wait to load or unload. Many pay plans pay you for that time after a set wait.

Not every plan pays detention. Your own pay plan decides.

What your plan may say

Look for these things in your pay plan or lease:

  • Free time. How long you wait before detention starts. It is often one or two hours.
  • The rate. How much you get for each hour, or part of an hour.
  • Rounding. Does a part of an hour count? Some plans count to the quarter hour. Some count only full hours.
  • A limit. Some plans stop paying after a set number of hours.
  • Proof. Some plans need a time stamp, a signed paper, or a call to dispatch.

How to check it

  1. Write down when you got there and when you left. Use your logbook or trip papers.
  2. Take away the free time.
  3. Round the way your plan says.
  4. Multiply the hours by the rate.

Made-up example: You waited 4 hours. Free time is 2 hours. That leaves 2 hours. At $20.00 an hour, that is $40.00.

  1. Find the detention line on your stub. Compare it with your number.

Why detention lines get missed

  • The time was not sent to payroll.
  • The free time or rounding works another way than you thought.
  • The detention is paid on a later stub.

If your number does not match, write down the stop, the times and your math. Then ask your payroll office. Keep a copy.
